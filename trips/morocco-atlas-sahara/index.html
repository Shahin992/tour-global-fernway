Atlas to Sahara

Riads and souks in Marrakech, two nights in a Berber guesthouse below Toubkal, the kasbah road south, and a night under the stars in the dunes of Erg Chebbi before the medina of Fes.

Why you’ll love it

  1. Mountains before desert. Two nights in an Imlil guesthouse and a day walking over the Tizi n’Tamatert pass, with lunch in a village home.
  2. A proper desert night. A small, family-run camp deep in the Erg Chebbi dunes, away from the generator-lit "luxury" camps near the road.
  3. Riads, not resorts. Courtyard houses in Marrakech and Fes, and a restored kasbah in Aït Benhaddou.
  4. The long road, done gently. The drive south is broken up with walks in the Dadès and Todra gorges, so no day is only a drive.
  5. Amazigh leaders. Our Morocco leaders come from the Atlas, speak Tamazight, Arabic, French and English, and are at home in every village you visit.

Day by day

  1. Day 1

    Marrakech: into the medina

    Arrive and settle into a riad a few minutes’ walk from Jemaa el-Fna. At dusk your leader takes the group into the square to eat at the food stalls the locals queue for.

    Meals: DStay: medina riad9 kg CO2e

  2. Day 2

    Marrakech: souks and gardens

    A morning with a local guide through the souks, the Ben Youssef Madrasa and a spice merchant’s family business. The afternoon is free for a hammam, the Majorelle Garden or a rooftop and a mint tea.

    Meals: BStay: medina riad7 kg CO2e

  3. Day 3

    Up into the High Atlas

    Ninety minutes by road to Imlil, at the foot of Toubkal. Bags go up by mule; you walk the last 30 minutes to the guesthouse, then take an afternoon walk to the village of Aroumd. 2 hours.

    Meals: B, L, DStay: Berber guesthouse14 kg CO2e

  4. Day 4

    Over the Tizi n’Tamatert pass

    A mule-supported walking day over the 2,279 m pass through walnut groves and terraced barley, with lunch in a family home in Tamatert. 10 km, 5 hours, 600 m of ascent.

    Meals: B, L, DStay: Berber guesthouse8 kg CO2e

  5. Day 5

    The Tizi n’Tichka pass to Aït Benhaddou

    Cross the High Atlas by the Tichka pass road to the earthen ksar of Aït Benhaddou. Walk through the old village at sunset when the day visitors have gone.

    Meals: B, DStay: kasbah guesthouse22 kg CO2e

  6. Day 6

    Skoura and the Dadès Gorge

    Through the palm groves of Skoura and the Valley of Roses to the Dadès Gorge, then an afternoon walk among the rust-red rock formations known as the monkey fingers. 2.5 hours.

    Meals: B, DStay: gorge-side guesthouse20 kg CO2e

  7. Day 7

    Todra Gorge to the dunes

    Walk the narrows of the Todra Gorge in the morning, then drive on to Merzouga. Ride a camel or walk for an hour into the Erg Chebbi dunes to a small camp; dinner, drums and a sky full of stars.

    Meals: B, DStay: desert camp (private tents, shared bathrooms)24 kg CO2e

  8. Day 8

    Sunrise, then north to Fes

    Climb a dune for sunrise, then the long drive north through the cedar forests of the Middle Atlas, where Barbary macaques live, to Fes (about seven hours with stops).

    Meals: B, DStay: Fes riad26 kg CO2e

  9. Day 9

    Fes el-Bali

    A morning in the world’s largest car-free medina: the Chouara tanneries, the Al-Qarawiyyin quarter and a bread oven that has served the same street for centuries. The trip ends after a farewell lunch.

    Meals: B, L6 kg CO2e

Before you book

Fitness

Walks are up to 5 hours on mule tracks, with one day of 600 m of ascent. A mule can carry anyone who tires. Expect three long road days of 5 to 7 hours with plenty of stops.

Dress

Morocco is relaxed but modest: covered shoulders and knees are respectful in villages and the medinas. Bring a warm layer; Atlas and desert nights are cold.

Entry requirements

Visa rules depend on your passport. Many nationalities can visit Morocco visa-free for up to 90 days; check your government’s advice and passport validity.

Season

We run spring, autumn and New Year departures. Summer in the Sahara regularly passes 45°C, so we don’t go then.

Questions about this trip

Do I have to ride a camel?

No. The ride into the dunes takes about an hour and many travellers prefer to walk alongside. The camels are owned by the family that runs our camp, and we check their care on every departure.

How cold is a night in the desert camp?

In spring and autumn, nights drop to 8 to 12°C, and to around 2°C at New Year. Tents have proper beds and heavy blankets, but bring a warm layer and a hat for the evening around the fire.

What should I wear in Morocco?

Light, loose clothes that cover shoulders and knees, comfortable walking shoes for the Atlas, and a fleece or down jacket for evenings. A scarf is useful for sun, dust and visiting religious sites.

How long are the drives?

Three days include 5 to 7 hours on the road, always broken by walks, lunch and photo stops. The minibus has air conditioning, and seats rotate so everyone gets a window.
