Kyoto & the Kumano Kodo

A temple night on Mount Kōya, four days on the thousand-year-old Nakahechi pilgrim trail, two nights in a hot-spring village, and the falls at Nachi at the end of it all.

Why you’ll love it

  1. The pilgrim trail, not the bus route. Four walking days on the Nakahechi, the imperial route to the Kumano shrines, through cedar forest and past a hundred tiny oji shrines.
  2. A night in a working temple. Monks’ vegetarian cooking on Mount Kōya, the 6am prayer service, and the lantern-lit Okunoin cemetery after dark.
  3. Hot springs every few days. Two nights in Yunomine Onsen, home of Tsuboyu, the only UNESCO-listed hot-spring bath in the world.
  4. Your bag goes ahead. On walking days your main luggage travels by van to the next inn, so you carry only a daypack.
  5. A leader from the Kii Peninsula. Our Kumano leaders grew up near the trail and know the innkeepers, the shrine priests and the best soba in Hongū.

Day by day

  1. Day 1

    Kyoto: welcome to the old city

    Arrive in Kyoto and check into a restored machiya townhouse in the Higashiyama district. In the evening your leader walks the group through lantern-lit Pontochō alley to a welcome dinner at a family izakaya.

    Meals: DStay: machiya guesthouse8 kg CO2e

  2. Day 2

    Kyoto: shrines before the crowds

    An early start for the upper torii paths of Fushimi Inari, which are almost empty before 8am. The afternoon is free; in the evening, a tea ceremony in a 19th-century tea house run by the same family for five generations.

    Meals: BStay: machiya guesthouse7 kg CO2e

  3. Day 3

    Mount Kōya: a night with the monks

    Train and cable car up to Kōyasan, the mountain monastery town founded in 816. Stay in shukubō temple lodgings, eat shōjin ryōri vegetarian cooking, and walk the 2 km cedar avenue of Okunoin cemetery by lantern light.

    Meals: B, DStay: temple lodging (shared bathrooms)10 kg CO2e

  4. Day 4

    To Tanabe, gateway to the trail

    After the morning prayer service, a private minibus takes the group down through the Kii mountains to Kii-Tanabe (about three hours). Evening in Ajikōji, Tanabe’s lane of tiny bars and grills.

    Meals: BStay: small city hotel16 kg CO2e

  5. Day 5

    Takijiri-ōji to Takahara

    The walking begins at Takijiri-ōji, the traditional entrance to the sacred Kumano area. A steep first hour climbs through forest to Takahara, the "village in the mists", with views over layered ridges. 4 km, 400 m of ascent, about 3 hours.

    Meals: B, L, DStay: Takahara lodge9 kg CO2e

  6. Day 6

    Takahara to Chikatsuyu

    The longest day: old stone paths, tea-house ruins and oji shrines through cedar and cypress forest to the riverside village of Chikatsuyu. 10 km, 550 m of ascent, 5 to 6 hours with a packed lunch on the trail.

    Meals: B, L, DStay: family minshuku9 kg CO2e

  7. Day 7

    Hosshinmon-ōji to Kumano Hongū Taisha

    A short transfer to Hosshinmon-ōji, then the classic final stretch to the grand shrine of Kumano Hongū Taisha through tea fields and hamlets. Continue by bus to Yunomine Onsen and bathe in Tsuboyu. 7 km, mostly downhill, about 3 hours.

    Meals: B, L, DStay: onsen ryokan9 kg CO2e

  8. Day 8

    Yunomine: a day at your own pace

    A rest day in the hot-spring village. Join your leader on the short Dainichi-goe trail to the giant torii at Ōyunohara, or take the traditional river boat down the Kumano-gawa (March to November).

    Meals: B, DStay: onsen ryokan7 kg CO2e

  9. Day 9

    Daimon-zaka to Nachi Falls

    Walk the moss-covered Daimon-zaka stone stairway, lined with 800-year-old cedars, to Kumano Nachi Taisha, the Seiganto-ji pagoda and the 133-metre Nachi Falls. Overnight by the sea in Kii-Katsuura. 2 km, 270 m of steps.

    Meals: B, DStay: seaside onsen hotel11 kg CO2e

  10. Day 10

    Limited express to Osaka

    An early look at Katsuura’s tuna auction, then the Kuroshio limited express along the coast to Osaka (about four hours). Farewell dinner in the backstreets of Shinsekai.

    Meals: B, DStay: Osaka hotel12 kg CO2e

  11. Day 11

    Osaka: journey’s end

    The trip ends after breakfast. Kansai International Airport is about 50 minutes away by train; your leader will help with tickets.

    Meals: B4 kg CO2e

Before you book

Fitness

You should be comfortable walking 5 to 6 hours with a daypack on uneven stone paths and steps, two or three days in a row. Walking poles help on the descents.

Rooms

Expect futons on tatami in traditional inns, and shared bathrooms at the temple and in Takahara. Private bathrooms elsewhere.

Entry requirements

Visa rules depend on your passport. Many nationalities enter Japan visa-free for short stays; check your government’s travel advice and your passport’s validity before booking.

Insurance

Travel insurance covering medical care, evacuation and cancellation is a condition of joining any Fernway trip.

Questions about this trip

How hard is the walking on the Kumano Kodo?

Moderate. The Nakahechi trail is well marked but steep in places, with stone steps and tree roots. The longest day is about 10 km with 550 m of climbing over 5 to 6 hours. If you walk a few hours most weekends, you will enjoy it.

Do I carry my own luggage on the trail?

No. On walking days your main bag is forwarded by van to the next inn. You carry a daypack with water, rain gear and lunch. We ask you to keep your main bag under 15 kg because inn staff carry it upstairs.

What is it like to stay in a ryokan or temple lodging?

You sleep on futons laid out on tatami mats, wear the yukata robe provided, and eat a multi-course dinner of local dishes. Baths are shared and separated by gender, with private family baths at most inns. Your leader explains the etiquette on day one.

When is the best time to walk the Kumano Kodo?

April, May, October and November. Spring brings cherry blossom in early April and fresh green forest; autumn has clear skies and colour in November. June is the rainy season and July and August are hot and humid, so we do not run group departures then.
