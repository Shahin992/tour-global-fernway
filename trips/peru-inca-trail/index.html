Sacred Valley & the Inca Trail

Two unhurried days acclimatising among weaving villages and salt terraces, then the classic four-day trail over Dead Woman’s Pass to the Sun Gate at first light.

Why you’ll love it

  1. Acclimatised properly. Four nights between 2,800 and 3,400 metres before the trail, so the high pass feels hard rather than frightening.
  2. The classic route, the classic way. All four days from Km 82, camping at the quieter sites and arriving through the Sun Gate as the light comes up.
  3. Porters who are paid and protected. Fair wages, insurance and a 20 kg load limit, checked by the trail authority and by us.
  4. A night with a family in Ollantaytambo. Dinner in an Inca-walled courtyard house that has been lived in for 500 years.
  5. Quechua-speaking leaders. Our Peru leaders grew up in the Sacred Valley and lead in English, Spanish and Quechua.

Day by day

  1. Day 1

    Cusco: arrive slowly

    Arrive in Cusco (3,400 m) and rest; the altitude is the first thing to respect. Coca tea in the courtyard, a short evening stroll in San Blas and a welcome dinner of Andean dishes.

    Meals: DStay: boutique hotel, San Blas9 kg CO2e

  2. Day 2

    Cusco on foot

    A gentle day: San Pedro market, the Inca sun temple of Qorikancha beneath the Santo Domingo church, and an afternoon walk up to the zigzag walls of Sacsayhuamán.

    Meals: B, LStay: boutique hotel, San Blas8 kg CO2e

  3. Day 3

    Chinchero, Moray and the Maras salt pans

    Visit a women’s weaving cooperative in Chinchero, the circular Inca terraces of Moray, then walk down past the 4,000 salt pans of Maras to the Urubamba river. About 2.5 hours, mostly downhill.

    Meals: B, LStay: valley lodge, Urubamba13 kg CO2e

  4. Day 4

    Pumamarca: the acclimatisation hike

    A quiet trail from Ollantaytambo climbs 400 metres to the Inca outpost of Pumamarca, a good test for the trail ahead. Dinner with a family in their Inca-walled home in Ollantaytambo.

    Meals: B, DStay: valley lodge, Urubamba10 kg CO2e

  5. Day 5

    Inca Trail day 1: Km 82 to Ayapata

    Permits checked at Piscacucho, then a steady day along the Urubamba past the Llactapata ruins. 12 km, 6 hours, 600 m of ascent. Your porters have camp set up when you arrive.

    Meals: B, L, DStay: camping18 kg CO2e

  6. Day 6

    Inca Trail day 2: Dead Woman’s Pass

    The big day. A long climb through cloud forest to Warmiwañusca, at 4,215 m the trail’s highest point, then a steep stone staircase down to camp. 11 km, 7 to 8 hours, 1,100 m up and 600 m down.

    Meals: B, L, DStay: camping18 kg CO2e

  7. Day 7

    Inca Trail day 3: the ruins day

    The most beautiful stretch: Runkurakay, Sayacmarca, Phuyupatamarca and the terraces of Wiñay Wayna, with views of the Vilcabamba snow peaks. 15 km, 8 hours, mostly downhill on Inca paving.

    Meals: B, L, DStay: camping18 kg CO2e

  8. Day 8

    Sun Gate and Machu Picchu

    A 3:30am start to be at the checkpoint when it opens, then the final hour to Inti Punku, the Sun Gate, for the first view of Machu Picchu. A guided tour of the citadel, then the bus, train and road back to Cusco.

    Meals: B, LStay: boutique hotel, Cusco21 kg CO2e

  9. Day 9

    Cusco: a free day

    Sleep in. Optional cooking class with a Cusco chef, or a visit to the textile centre. Farewell dinner together in the evening.

    Meals: B, DStay: boutique hotel, Cusco8 kg CO2e

  10. Day 10

    Cusco: journey’s end

    The trip ends after breakfast. Airport transfers are included for any flight leaving today.

    Meals: B5 kg CO2e

Before you book

Fitness

You need to be a regular walker who can manage 7 to 8 hours on steep steps at altitude. Train with long hill days and stair climbs for two to three months beforehand.

Permits

Inca Trail permits are issued in your passport name and cannot be transferred or changed. We need your passport details when you book, and we buy permits the same week.

Altitude

Talk to your doctor before booking if you have heart or lung conditions. Our leaders carry oxygen and a pulse oximeter, and check everyone each morning.

Entry requirements

Visa rules depend on your passport. Many nationalities can visit Peru without a visa for tourism; check your government’s advice and passport validity.

Questions about this trip

How hard is the Inca Trail?

Challenging but achievable for regular walkers. The trail is about 42 km over four days, and day two climbs 1,100 metres to 4,215 m on uneven stone steps. Fitness matters, but pacing and acclimatisation matter more; we walk slowly and start early.

Will I get altitude sickness?

Most people feel mild symptoms in Cusco, such as headaches or breathlessness, which ease within a day or two. That is why this trip spends four nights at altitude before the trail. Drink plenty of water, avoid alcohol for the first two days, and ask your doctor about medication.

When do Inca Trail permits sell out?

The government releases a limited number of permits each year, and dates from May to August often sell out within weeks of release early in the year. Booking six to nine months ahead gives you the best choice.

Is the Inca Trail open all year?

It closes every February for maintenance. The dry season, May to September, has the clearest skies; April and October are greener and quieter, with a chance of afternoon showers.
