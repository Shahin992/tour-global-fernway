About Fernway

We started with one trail and twelve chairs.

Fernway was founded by two trekking leaders who were tired of 40-seat coaches and hotel buffets. The idea was simple: one table, twelve chairs, and a leader who lives where you are going.

Our story

Fewer people, longer lunches.

In 2009, Anna Okafor and Tomás Reyes were leading treks for large tour companies: Anna in the Atlas, Tomás in the Andes. They noticed the same thing on every trip. The best moments happened when the group was small enough to be invited in, to a kitchen, a festival or a shepherd’s hut, and the itinerary was loose enough to say yes.

Fernway began that year with a single Atlas departure for nine travellers. Today we run eleven small-group trips and plan tailor-made journeys in thirty-one countries, but the rules have not changed: never more than twelve, leaders who live in the region, and days built around walking, eating and meeting people.

We are still small. Our planning team is twenty-two people across six time zones, and every trip on our website has been walked by someone in the office.

Our footprint

A number on every trip, and what we do about it.

Each itinerary shows an estimated footprint in kilograms of CO2e per traveller per day. Here is how that figure is made, and what we do with it.

  1. Measure

    We add up accommodation (from each hotel’s energy data, or a regional average), ground transport by distance and vehicle, meals and activities, then divide by travellers and days.

  2. Exclude nothing hidden

    International flights are excluded only because they depend on where you start. We show them separately when you book, using your actual route.

  3. Reduce

    Trains instead of internal flights, local and seasonal menus, refill stations instead of bottles, and longer stays so vehicles drive less.

  4. Contribute

    $25 of every booking goes to a conservation or community project in the region you visit, chosen with our leaders and reported each year.

What we won’t do

Some easy money we leave on the table.

  • No groups over twelve, even when a departure is oversubscribed. We add a date instead.
  • No riding elephants, no orphanage visits, and no wildlife encounters that involve handling animals.
  • No commission shopping stops. If we take you to a workshop, it is because it is good.
  • No hidden extras. The price you book is the price you pay, with no fuel surcharges.

About us

Two questions about how we work.

Who leads Fernway trips? Are the guides local?

Every group trip has a Fernway leader who lives in the region: 92% of our 64 leaders guide within a day’s drive of home. Where the law requires it, such as on the Inca Trail or in national parks, they work alongside licensed specialist guides.

How do you measure a trip’s carbon footprint?

We estimate emissions from accommodation, ground transport, meals and activities, then divide by the number of travellers and days to give kg CO2e per traveller per day. International flights are shown separately because they depend on your starting point. The method is reviewed each year.
